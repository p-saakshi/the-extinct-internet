1\. Basic Identity
------------------

**Scientific name:** Triceratops\
**Display name:** Triceratops\
**Taxon rank:** Genus\
**Common name:** Triceratops

### Notes

For V1, I recommend keeping the account at the **genus level** instead of forcing it into one species.

The two commonly recognized species are:

-   *Triceratops horridus*
-   *Triceratops prorsus*

Research from the Hell Creek Formation suggests that these species occur at different stratigraphic levels rather than being completely interchangeable. *T. horridus* is found lower in the formation, while *T. prorsus* occurs higher.

So for our app:

**Account identity:** `Triceratops`\
**Taxon rank:** `genus`

That keeps the character scientifically cleaner.

7\. Scientific Relationships
----------------------------

### Triceratops → T. rex

**Relationship type:** Possible predator-prey / ecosystem interaction\
**Confidence:** Moderate to high

Tyrannosaur bite marks have been reported on Triceratops fossils.

Some Triceratops injuries show healing, which indicates the animal survived at least some traumatic encounters.

However, bite marks alone cannot always distinguish between:

-   active predation
-   scavenging
-   feeding on an already-dead animal

So for our app:

**Allowed:**\
"T. rex and Triceratops could plausibly know each other."

**Not allowed as scientific fact:**\
"T. rex hunted Triceratops every day."

* * * * *

### Triceratops → Edmontosaurus

**Relationship type:** Ecosystem contemporary\
**Confidence:** High

Both lived in the Hell Creek ecosystem during the latest Cretaceous.

They were both herbivores and could plausibly have encountered one another.

* * * * *

### Triceratops → Ankylosaurus

**Relationship type:** Ecosystem contemporary\
**Confidence:** High

Both occur in the same broad Late Cretaceous ecosystem and overlap strongly in time.

No specific direct ecological interaction needs to be invented.

* * * * *

### Triceratops → Triceratops

**Relationship type:** Possible intraspecific competition / social interaction\
**Confidence:** Moderate

Some injuries and damage patterns on Triceratops skulls and frills have been interpreted as evidence that individuals may have used their horns during combat or social interactions.

The exact behavior is uncertain.

For personality purposes later, this gives us room for:

-   territorial behavior
-   rivalry
-   stubbornness
-   social tension

without pretending we know exactly how they behaved.

9\. Uncertainty / Disputed Information
--------------------------------------

| Topic | What is uncertain? | How should the app handle it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Species identity | *T. horridus* and *T. prorsus* occur at different stratigraphic levels | Use genus-level `Triceratops` account |
| Exact mass | Estimates vary between specimens and studies | Use approximate range |
| Herding | Large permanent herds are not firmly established | Avoid confident herd claims |
| Horn function | Defense, display, combat, or combinations thereof | Keep wording flexible |
| T. rex predation frequency | Bite evidence exists, but not every bite means an active hunt | Say predation was plausible, not constant |
| Individual familiarity | Species-level coexistence does not prove particular individuals knew each other | Treat personal familiarity as fictional worldbuilding |
