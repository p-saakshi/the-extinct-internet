3\. Geological Time
-------------------

**Era:** Cenozoic\
**Period:** Quaternary\
**Epoch:** Pleistocene, with isolated populations surviving into the Holocene

This is the first V1 creature where our simple `max_ma` / `min_ma` representation gets interesting.

Woolly mammoths existed for hundreds of thousands of years, but the **very last surviving population on Wrangel Island persisted until roughly 4,000 years ago**.

### Recommended V1 numerical range

**Oldest known range --- max_ma:** approximately 0.4 Ma\
**Youngest known range --- min_ma:** approximately 0.004 Ma

In easier terms:

-   about **400,000 years ago**
-   until about **4,000 years ago**

### Important note

Most woolly mammoths disappeared much earlier than the Wrangel Island population.

So the genus/species-wide range does **not** mean a mammoth living 100,000 years ago personally coexisted with one living 4,000 years ago.

For our V1 world logic, this broad species range is fine, but geography and population context still matter.

4\. Ecology
-----------

**Diet:** Herbivore

**Feeding strategy:** Grazing and browsing herbivore adapted primarily to cold steppe-tundra environments.

**Locomotion:** Quadrupedal

**Body size:** Roughly comparable in overall size to modern large elephants; body size varied by sex, population and individual. The Smithsonian describes the woolly mammoth as roughly comparable in size to a modern African elephant.

**Habitat / environment summary:**\
Cold, dry, open grassland and steppe-tundra environments across northern Eurasia and North America.

A major habitat associated with woolly mammoths is the **mammoth steppe**, a vast Pleistocene biome dominated largely by grasses, sedges and herbaceous plants. It stretched across enormous portions of Eurasia and into North America.

### Feeding Notes

The woolly mammoth was strongly associated with open vegetation.

Likely foods included:

-   grasses
-   sedges
-   herbs
-   other low-growing vegetation
-   some shrubs and woody plants depending on habitat and season

Its broad ecological identity for our chatbot should therefore be:

**cold-adapted grazing/browsing megaherbivore**

rather than "it only ate grass."

* * * * *

5\. Cold-Climate Adaptations
----------------------------

This is probably the most important biological section for the character.

Woolly mammoths were adapted to extremely cold northern environments.

Important adaptations included:

-   long outer hair
-   dense insulating underfur
-   substantial subcutaneous fat
-   relatively small ears compared with modern elephants
-   short tail
-   large tusks
-   a high-domed head
-   cold-adapted body proportions

These traits reduced heat loss and helped the animal survive in Pleistocene steppe-tundra climates.

### Tusks

Woolly mammoths possessed large, strongly curved tusks.

Tusks could plausibly have been involved in:

-   manipulating vegetation
-   moving snow
-   social display
-   competition
-   defense

But we should avoid making one single function sound certain.

7\. The Wrangel Island Population
---------------------------------

This is one of the coolest facts in the entire V1 roster.

Most mainland woolly mammoths disappeared around the end of the Pleistocene and beginning of the Holocene.

But a population became isolated on **Wrangel Island**, in the Arctic Ocean north of Siberia.

That population survived until only around **4,000 years ago**.

The classic 1993 Nature paper reported Wrangel mammoth remains dating from roughly **7,000 to 4,000 years before present**.

That means the last woolly mammoths were alive **thousands of years after monumental human civilizations had already begun developing elsewhere**.

This is a fantastic chatbot fact, but phrase it carefully:

**Safe:**\
"Some of my species were still around about 4,000 years ago."

**Not safe:**\
"I watched ancient Egyptians build pyramids."

They were geographically nowhere near Egypt.

* * * * *

8\. Humans and Woolly Mammoths
------------------------------

Unlike every dinosaur in our V1 roster, the woolly mammoth **actually overlapped with humans**.

This creates a major difference in our knowledge system.

Humans interacted with mammoths in the Pleistocene through:

-   hunting
-   scavenging
-   use of mammoth bones and ivory
-   shared landscapes

However, mammoth extinction should **not** be represented as simply:

> humans killed all the mammoths.

Research suggests woolly mammoth decline and extinction involved interacting factors including:

-   climatic change
-   habitat transformation
-   population fragmentation
-   human presence and hunting pressure

The relative importance of these factors varied by region and time.

So the chatbot should not reduce extinction to a single cause.

11\. Social Behavior
--------------------

Because woolly mammoths are close relatives of elephants, it is tempting to copy modern elephant society directly onto them.

We should be more cautious.

Evidence suggests mammoths were social animals, and research on fossil trackways and demographic patterns supports group living.

But details such as:

-   exact dominance hierarchy
-   lifelong family organization
-   matriarchal structure identical to modern elephants
-   specific emotional rituals

should not automatically be asserted as fact.

For the character, **social, group-oriented behavior is plausible**, but detailed elephant-style society should remain cautious worldbuilding.

13\. Uncertainty / Disputed Information
---------------------------------------

| Topic | What is uncertain? | How should the app handle it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact species origin date | Estimates differ depending on fossil/genetic evidence | Use approximately 400 ka for V1 rather than false precision |
| Extinction cause | Climate, habitat change and humans all contributed in complex ways | Never blame one cause as settled fact |
| Exact diet | Diet varied geographically and seasonally | Describe it broadly as grazing/browsing |
| Social organization | Group behavior is plausible, but exact structure is uncertain | Don't copy modern elephant society wholesale |
| Tusks | Probably had multiple functions | Don't claim one exclusive purpose |
| Wrangel extinction | Final extinction may have been rapid, but exact immediate trigger remains debated | Treat exact cause as uncertain |
| Individual human contact | Species overlapped with humans, but not every mammoth encountered people | Separate species-level possibility from individual lore |
| "Pyramid mammoths" | Mammoths and early civilizations overlapped chronologically, not geographically | Never claim mammoths lived alongside Egyptians |
