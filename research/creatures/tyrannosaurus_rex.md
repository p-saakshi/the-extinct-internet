## 4. Ecology

**Diet:** Carnivore

**Feeding strategy:** Active predator and scavenger; capable of consuming and crushing bone.

**Locomotion:** Bipedal

**Estimated minimum length (m):** approximately 11
**Estimated maximum length (m):** approximately 13

**Estimated minimum mass (kg):** approximately 7,000
**Estimated maximum mass (kg):** over 8,000 for exceptionally large individuals

**Habitat / environment summary:**
Warm, humid, heavily vegetated terrestrial environments including river systems and broad floodplains. The Hell Creek landscape has been compared to a modern Louisiana-style floodplain, with abundant plant life supporting diverse dinosaur, mammal, reptile, insect and other animal communities.

### Feeding notes

Fossil evidence shows that T. rex was not simply an obligate scavenger.

A T. rex tooth has been found embedded in healed hadrosaur vertebrae. Because the surrounding bone healed after the injury, the hadrosaur survived the attack for some time. This provides direct evidence that T. rex attacked living prey.

Biomechanical studies also indicate that adult T. rex possessed exceptionally powerful jaws capable of deeply penetrating and fragmenting bone.

T. rex likely both hunted and scavenged, similar to many modern large predators.

### Notes

Hell Creek fossil surveys document Tyrannosaurus alongside Triceratops, Edmontosaurus and Ankylosaurus.

Triceratops was especially abundant in the Hell Creek fauna.

Edmontosaurus was also common and direct fossil evidence exists of T. rex attacking a hadrosaur closely comparable to Edmontosaurus annectens.

Ankylosaurus is much rarer in the fossil record but occurs in the same formation.

## 7. Scientific Relationships

### T. rex → Edmontosaurus

**Relationship type:** Possible / supported predator-prey relationship
**Confidence:** High

A fossil from the Hell Creek Formation preserves a T. rex tooth embedded in healed hadrosaur tail vertebrae.

The animal survived long enough for bone growth to occur around the wound, meaning the bite happened while it was alive.

The researchers compare the hadrosaur material with Edmontosaurus annectens.

* * * * *

### T. rex → Triceratops

**Relationship type:** Possible predator-prey / feeding relationship\
**Confidence:** Moderate to high

Tyrannosaurid bite traces have been reported on Triceratops fossils, including healed injuries.

However, not every bite mark can conclusively distinguish active predation from scavenging.

For chatbot purposes, it is scientifically reasonable to portray T. rex and Triceratops as animals that could encounter one another and potentially have predator-prey interactions.

The bot should not claim that every T. rex routinely hunted Triceratops.

* * * * *

### T. rex → Ankylosaurus

**Relationship type:** Ecosystem contemporary\
**Confidence:** High

Both occur in the Hell Creek Formation.

There is not enough evidence for V1 to confidently claim a specific recurring predator-prey relationship between them.

For chatbot purposes:

```

```

### T. rex → T. rex

**Relationship type:** Intraspecific feeding / possible cannibalism\
**Confidence:** High

Several T. rex fossils preserve feeding traces attributed to other T. rex individuals.

This provides evidence that T. rex sometimes fed on members of its own species.

The exact circumstances are uncertain and may include scavenging rather than active killing.

9\. Uncertainty / Disputed Information
--------------------------------------

| Topic | What is uncertain? | How should the app handle it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact body size | Individual specimens vary considerably | Use approximate ranges rather than one exact size |
| Hunting vs scavenging | T. rex clearly hunted, but also likely scavenged | Describe it as both predator and scavenger |
| Pack hunting | Evidence is uncertain | Bot must not confidently claim T. rex hunted in packs |
| Feathers | Direct evidence for extensive feather covering in adult T. rex is lacking | Avoid strong claims unless relevant |
| Triceratops predation frequency | Bite evidence exists, but not every trace proves active predation | Say predation was possible, not guaranteed |
| Ankylosaurus predation | Shared ecosystem is well supported, direct interaction is less certain | Allow familiarity but avoid invented hunting stories |
| Individual encounters | Fossils show species-level coexistence, not specific individuals knowing one another | Treat personal familiarity as fictional worldbuilding built on plausible coexistence |
